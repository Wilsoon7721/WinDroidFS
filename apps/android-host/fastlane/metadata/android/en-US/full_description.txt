WinDroidFS gives your phone its own drive letter in Windows File Explorer. Open, copy and move files the same way you would on your own local drives, without MTP.

You need the WinDroidFS app for Windows too. Download it from https://windroidfs.wilsoon.dev or the GitHub releases page.

<b>4 ways to connect</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Direct USB:</b> plug your phone in. No setup on the phone.</li>
<li><b>Local Wi-Fi:</b> pair once, then connect without a cable.</li>
<li><b>USB tethering:</b> for when Direct USB is blocked.</li>
<li><b>ADB forwarding:</b> for developers, and the only way to reach Android/data.</li>
</ul>

<b>A real drive</b>
Any program can open files on it, and robocopy, move, xcopy and drag-and-drop all work. Copying 1,000 mixed files (150 MB) onto the phone over USB 2.0 takes about 19 seconds.

<b>You decide what the PC sees</b>
<ul>
<li>Share all of your storage, or only the folders you tick, like DCIM and Download.</li>
<li>Turn on Require biometrics and a PC sees nothing until you confirm on the phone, even one you've paired before.</li>
<li>Keep the screen awake while a PC is connected, so transfers don't stop when the phone locks.</li>
</ul>

<b>Stays on your network</b>
The phone and PC talk directly over the cable or your Wi-Fi. No account, no cloud, no tracking.

<b>Why All Files Access?</b>
WinDroidFS shows your storage as a drive, so it needs to read and write every file you choose to share, not just photos and media. Nothing leaves your phone except to the PC you connect.

WinDroidFS is open source under the GPL-3.0.
